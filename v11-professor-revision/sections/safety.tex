\section{RQ2: Safety of Carry-Forward}\label{sec:safety}
Safety is a defining property of \sys{}, so we report it as a family of rates rather than as a single count. For a set of targets with reference classes, let $C$ be the targets whose code was edited or is gone, $P$ the content-preserved targets, $S$ the targets that survive and $A$ all targets, and let $R=\{\lbl{changed},\lbl{removed}\}$ be the labels that request re-check. Then
\begin{align}
\text{missed re-check} &= |\{c\in C : \ell(c)\notin R\}|\,/\,|C|,\label{eq:missed}\\
\text{false re-check} &= |\{p\in P : \ell(p)\in R\}|\,/\,|P|,\\
\text{silent misplacement} &= |\{x\in W : \ell(x)\notin R\}|\,/\,|A|,
\end{align}
where $W\subseteq A$ are the targets placed at a wrong location, and the lost rate is the share of $S$ for which no location is returned. The re-check rule is the deployed one: \lbl{changed} and \lbl{removed} request re-check; \lbl{identical}, \lbl{content-match} and \lbl{moved} keep the issue's state. Earlier versions of this work scored every label other than the deployed \texttt{exact} as flagged; that adapter counted a \lbl{moved} label as a re-check and therefore could not observe the failures below. Placement (file and start line on surviving targets) and classification (label against reference class) are scored separately, because a location can be right while its change status is wrong.

\subsection{Missed Re-checks Are Rare but Not Absent}
On controlled edits and real histories, no observation receives a \lbl{content-match} label when the code inside its quoted span changed; edits around the span are outside this check, as the applied review suggestions show. That is true but incomplete. On real histories, \smRealCasMissK{} of \smRealCasMissN{} edited or gone targets (\smRealCasMissPct\%; Wilson 95\% interval \smRealCasMissLo--\smRealCasMissHi\%) are not returned for re-check, and \smRealCasSilentK{} of \smRealCasSilentN{} targets (\smRealCasSilentPct\%) are placed on the wrong code without a request. On the controlled main seed the corresponding counts are \smCtlCasMissK{} of \smCtlCasMissN{} and \smCtlCasSilentK{} of \smCtlCasSilentN{}. The cascade never requested re-check for preserved real-history content (\smRealCasFalseK{} of \smRealCasFalseN{}); its cost on real histories is instead \smRealCasLostPct\% lost anchors, which request re-check and leave the supervisor to search manually.

Every one of the \smRealCasMissK{} real-history misses carries the \lbl{moved} label (Table~\ref{tab:missedcases}). In \wpaMissCross{} cases tier~4 found the quotation in another file and in \wpaMissSame{} cases tier~2 found it elsewhere in the same file, while the original had been edited (\wpaMissEdited{}) or deleted (\wpaMissGone{}). The mechanism is a pre-existing identical copy: the matcher follows the text, and the text still exists. \wpaMissSingleLine{} of the \wpaMissTotal{} anchors are a single line, the kind of short statement most likely to recur verbatim. This is the identical-code ambiguity anticipated in Section~\ref{sec:design}, and it is the dominant safety failure of the method; Section~\ref{sec:stress} shows that it becomes frequent when copies are common.

\subsection{What a Missed Re-check Means for the Supervisor}
A missed re-check does not hide an issue. The issue stays active and visible in the new review, with its carried quotation, its new location and the plain-language note ``the code it points to moved; it is shown at its new place''. What is lost is the prompt: the issue is not marked \lbl{needs re-check}, and the location it shows is a copy of the old code, so a supervisor who trusts the location sees the old code apparently unchanged. A silent misplacement is the same failure seen from the location side. Whether supervisors notice such cases was not measured. The interface's only mitigation is that the carried quotation is shown beside the new location. That quotation is the text at the previous version, not necessarily the one the supervisor reviewed (Section~\ref{sec:integrity}), and showing it does not replace an empirical check.

\subsection{Scope of the Guarantee}
The state contract guarantees, for the evaluated implementation, that no system action marks an issue \lbl{resolved}; Section~\ref{sec:statecheck} verifies this against every status write in the code. It does not guarantee that every change is detected or that every anchor is placed correctly. Those are empirical properties of the matcher, bounded by the rates above for the evaluated distributions only.

\subsection{Safety--Utility Trade-off}
Configurations trade the outcomes against one another rather than improving all of them. Lowering the threshold to 0.5 cuts real-history lost anchors from \smRealCasLostPct\% to \smRealCalLostPct\% and missed re-checks from \smRealCasMissPct\% to \smRealCalMissPct\%, but raises wrong placements from \smRealCasWrongPct\% to \smRealCalWrongPct\%, nearly all of which do request re-check. The contextual tracker has the lowest real-history missed and silent rates of the evaluated matchers but cannot follow code between files. Under an additive cost model with the cost of a lost anchor fixed at one and log-spaced weights for the other outcomes, no method minimizes cost across the whole weight range (Fig.~\ref{fig:effort}); the ranking changes with the relative price of a silent misplacement and a manual search. We therefore state the deployment objective explicitly: minimize missed re-checks and silent misplacements first, and only then manual searches and unnecessary re-checks. Under that objective the guards of Section~\ref{sec:guards}, not a lower threshold, are the relevant intervention. The weights are illustrative; no faculty cost was measured.
