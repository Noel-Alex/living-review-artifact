\section{RQ2: Does the Safety Layer Keep Changed Code in Front of the Supervisor?}\label{sec:layer}

\subsection{How Carry-Forward Fails}
On controlled edits and real histories no observation received a \lbl{content-match} label when the code inside its quoted span had changed, but on real histories \smRealCasMissK{} of \smRealCasMissN{} edited or gone targets (\smRealCasMissPct\%; Wilson interval \smRealCasMissLo--\smRealCasMissHi\%) were not returned for re-check, and \smRealCasSilentK{} of \smRealCasSilentN{} targets were placed on wrong code without a request. Every miss carries the \lbl{moved} label: tier~4 found the quotation in another file (\wpaMissCross{} cases) or tier~2 elsewhere in the same file (\wpaMissSame), while the original had been edited or deleted. The mechanism is a pre-existing identical copy; \wpaMissSingleLine{} of the \wpaMissTotal{} anchors are a single line. The cascade requested no unnecessary re-check on preserved real-history code; its cost is \smRealCasLostPct\% lost anchors.

\subsection{The Layer Over Other Locators}
Table~\ref{tab:locator} passes each locator's window through the labels alone and then through IG+AGi; the guards never move a window, so each locator keeps its placement (VW belongs to the cascade and changes placement only in its guarded rows). The labels alone already turn a window into a request decision: CodeMapper with labels only missed \wiRealCmQMissK{} of the \wiRealCmQMissN{} needed re-checks on real histories. The guards' increment is small on natural code and large where copies and indentation changes are common: over CodeMapper, IG+AGi cut missed re-checks from \wiRealCmQMissK{} to \wiRealCmGiMissK{} on real histories, from \wiStuCmQMissK{} to \wiStuCmGiMissK{} of \wiStuCmQMissN{} on the student repositories and from \wiHumCmQMissK{} to \wiHumCmGiMissK{} of \wiHumCmQMissN{} on the human-annotated regions, but from \wiFrCmQMiss\% to \wiFrCmGiMiss\% on the stress seed (paired cluster interval \wiFrCmGiVsCmQMissLo{} to \wiFrCmGiVsCmQMissHi{} points). The contextual tracker responded in the same way. IG supplies the indentation cases, which no locator reports (CodeMapper missed \wiFrIndCmQMiss\% of the behavior-changing ones, guarded \wiFrIndCmGiMiss\%), and AGi the windows that land on an identical copy. Tables~\ref{tab:external} and~\ref{tab:locator} differ in guard version (AG against AGi) and in metric (correct outcome against placement), not in data.

The corrected copy guard AGi and the first one, AG, give the same placements and differ only in which windows they flag. With IG, AGi asked for fewer unnecessary re-checks than AG over CodeMapper on every population (stress seed \wiFrCmGiFalse\% against \wiFrCmGFalse\%; student repositories \wiStuCmGiFalse\% against \wiStuCmGFalse\%), but it missed more where an identical copy sits just below the original, because the line diff then aligns the original's lines with the copy (duplicate suite \wiFrDupCmGiMiss\% against \wiFrDupCmGMiss\%; human-annotated regions \wiHumCmGiMissK{} against \wiHumCmGMissK{}). The provenance rule AG2 removed still more requests but failed exactly where a copy guard is needed (duplicate suite: \wiFrDupCasAGtwoMiss\% missed, against \wiFrDupCasAGiMiss\% with AGi and \wiFrDupDepMiss\% with no guard), a negative result.

\subsection{A Language Model as the Locator}\label{sec:llm}
The portal already calls a language model, so we asked whether a model's own judgment of change can replace the layer. It cannot. Judging change itself, \wjModel{} left \wjPubSelfMissK{} of \wjPubSelfMissN{} needed re-checks unrequested on the open-source histories, \wjStuSelfMissK{} of \wjStuSelfMissN{} on the student repositories and \wjHumSelfMissK{} of \wjHumSelfMissN{} on the human-annotated regions, and placed \wjPubSelfSilentK{}, \wjStuSelfSilentK{} and \wjHumSelfSilentK{} targets on wrong code without a request. Comparing the quoted text at the model's window instead of trusting its answer closed most of that gap on the open-source rows (to \wjPubQMissK{} misses) and part of it on the student rows (to \wjStuQMissK{}), but not on the human-annotated regions (\wjHumQMissK{}), where only the guards helped; with IG+AGi the model left \wjPubGiMissK{}, \wjStuGiMissK{} and \wjHumGiMissK{} misses. J1 and J2 met the registered rule, a paired cluster interval excluding zero, on all three populations, but after Holm's adjustment over \wjHolmTests{} exact tests only the open-source pair stays below 0.05 (\wjPubJOneHolm{}, \wjPubJTwoHolm{}), so the small human-annotated result is consistent, not established.

The secondary model, \wjNemoModel{}, judged change more cautiously (\wjNemoPubSelfMissK{} open-source misses against \wjPubSelfMissK{}), and the layer again closed most of the remaining gap (\wjNemoPubGiMissK{}, \wjNemoStuGiMissK{} and \wjNemoHumGiMissK{} misses). Temperature~0 did not make \wjModel{} deterministic: a repeat of the human-annotated run gave the same placement, range and label for \wjRepSameAll{} of \wjRepN{} regions.

\subsection{An Independent Multi-Language Oracle}\label{sec:suggest}
The references above are made by us or, for the human-annotated regions, small. A GitHub review suggestion carries two anchors: the lines it quotes, $O$, and the replacement the reviewer proposes, $S$. Where they occur at the pull request's last head gives an oracle that needs no annotator: \emph{applied} ($S$ occurs once and $O$ no longer, so the concern was acted on and its code is at $S$), \emph{untouched} ($O$ once, $S$ absent), \emph{rewritten} (neither), \emph{deleted}, and \emph{ambiguous} (several occurrences). Under a registered protocol we took \wkUnits{} suggestions on source files from \wkPRs{} merged pull requests in \wkRepos{} repositories, two each in Python, TypeScript, Go, Rust and Java, chosen by star rank and licence: \wkClsApplied{} were applied, \wkClsUntouched{} untouched, \wkClsRewritten{} rewritten, \wkClsGone{} deleted and \wkClsAmbiguous{} ambiguous. Two independently written classifiers agreed on every suggestion. The scope is the file pair, so moves between files are not exercised (tier E4; CodeMapper also E5).

CodeMapper placed \wkCmQPlace\% of the applied or untouched targets correctly, the deployed cascade \wkDepPlace\% and GitHub \wkGhPlace\%, because GitHub gives up the position of every outdated thread (Table~\ref{tab:suggest}). GitHub's flag, read as it is now, also left \wkGhMiss\% of the changed targets marked current. Every missed re-check of the cascade and of CodeMapper, guarded or not, came from one mechanism that the earlier populations did not show (GitHub's flag, which follows the diff hunk rather than the quotation, also missed rewritten and applied suggestions): the suggestion was applied by adding lines around the reviewed lines, which stayed verbatim (\wkAppliedWrap{} of the \wkClsApplied{} applied suggestions, for example a new guard clause around an unchanged call). The change is real, but no comparison of the quoted text can see it, so S1 holds only for changes inside the quoted span. AG reduced CodeMapper's misses from \wkCmQMissK{} to \wkCmAgMissK{} at the price of more requests on untouched code (\wkCmQExtra\% to \wkCmAgExtra\%); AGi changed neither count of misses. No locator placed a target on wrong code silently; placement errors came with a request (Misdir \wkDepMisdir\% for the cascade and \wkCmQMisdir\% for CodeMapper).

The oracle also tests what a model should be asked. On \wkLlmN{} applied or untouched suggestions whose comment has at least four words, sampled per language and class, two hosted models were asked either whether the code changed in a way that could matter to a reviewer, the question of Section~\ref{sec:llm}, or whether the reviewer's concern was addressed, given the comment. Taking application as the reference for an addressed concern, the concern question reached precision \wkBeamClaimPrecision\% and \wkNemoClaimPrecision\% (Beam, Nemotron) and recall \wkBeamClaimRecall\% and \wkNemoClaimRecall\%, against \wkBeamDiffPrecision\% and \wkNemoDiffPrecision\% and \wkBeamDiffRecall\% and \wkNemoDiffRecall\% for the change question (exact McNemar $p$ \wkBeamMcNemarP{} for both models). It also recognized all \wkBeamWrapN{} sampled applications that wrap the quotation, which no locator signals. It still called \wkBeamClaimFP{} and \wkNemoClaimFP{} of the \wkLlmUntouched{} untouched suggestions addressed. On this sample, then, the concern question predicts whether a suggestion was applied better than the change question does; application is a proxy for an addressed concern, not a judgment of one, so the result supports showing such an assessment beside a request without showing that it helps supervisors. It may only order or annotate the queue, never resolve or keep an issue (S4).

\subsection{Copies by Number and Distance}\label{sec:copysuite}
A held-out parametric suite (tier E3) varies what a copy guard depends on. Its \wpNMain{} constructed trials cross 0, 1, 2, 4 or 8 pre-existing identical copies with the distance of the nearest one, anchors of 1--8 lines and the fate of the original; \wpNSame{} more trials let the revision itself add the copy (Table~\ref{tab:copysuite}). Without guards the deployed cascade missed \wpDepMiss\% of the changes, since any copy is found and reported \lbl{moved}. Distance decided the outcome and the number of copies did not: the guarded cascade missed \wpGiCOneMiss\% of changes with one copy and \wpGiCEightMiss\% with eight, and every miss of a guarded locator had a copy directly next to the original (\wpGiDAdjMiss\% of those changes for the cascade, \wpCmGiDAdjMiss\% for CodeMapper). An adjacent copy sits where the diff predicts the original, and the guards inspect only placements away from the predicted start. Over the cascade the guards turned misses into misdirected requests (Misdir \wpGiMisdir\%), not correct placements; CodeMapper placed every surviving original correctly.

AG and AGi missed the same pre-existing copies and differ in two conditions. AG requested a check on every original shifted by lines inserted directly above it (\wpAgFShiftExtra\%), AGi on none (\wpGiFShiftExtra\%). When the revision adds the copy, both are blind by construction, but AGi missed more: \wpGiSameMiss\% against \wpAgSameMiss\% over the cascade and \wpCmGiSameMiss\% against \wpCmAgSameMiss\% over CodeMapper (paired difference \wpSameDCmGiVCmAgMiss{} points, \wpSameDCmGiVCmAgMissLo{} to \wpSameDCmGiVCmAgMissHi). Grouped into issues of two to four anchors, fewer changes went unreturned (\wpCmGiIssueMiss\% of issues for CodeMapper+IG+AGi), because another anchor of the same issue often requested a check.

Anchor length acts in opposite directions on natural and constructed code. On real histories one-line anchors carried \wyRealOneDepMissK{} of the deployed cascade's \smRealCasMissK{} misses, and the guards left \wyRealOneGiMissK{} of them over the cascade and \wyRealOneCmGiMissK{} over CodeMapper. On the stress seed the guards' residual misses grew with length instead, from \wyFrOneCmGiMiss\% of changed one-line anchors to \wyFrTenCmGiMiss\% of anchors of ten or more lines over CodeMapper (cascade \wyFrOneGiMiss\% and \wyFrTenGiMiss\%). Split by the cascade's tier, its only confidence signal, and by whether a guard fired, every real-history miss of the guarded cascade came from an exact-match tier on which no guard fired (\wyRealQuietMiss), and of the \wyRealFiredN{} targets on which a guard fired \wyRealFiredChanged{} had changed and \wyRealFiredExtra{} were unnecessary requests.

\subsection{Ten Successive Commits}\label{sec:prefixes}
Errors can accumulate when an updated anchor is carried repeatedly. We replayed each ten-commit horizon of the real histories hop by hop and re-ran the reference from each chain's first commit to commits 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10; \bTwoChainEvery{} targets were decidable at every prefix and \bTwoDecidableTen{} at commit 10, the denominator of the endpoints. These intermediate references are the same heuristic, not independent identity labels, and the replay assumes no supervisor action. Endpoint accuracy of the deployed cascade fell from \bTwoSeqAccOne\% after one commit to \bTwoSeqAccTen\% after ten, mostly through lost anchors. Because a request persists in the issue's state, the relevant measure is cumulative: of the targets changed by commit 10, \bTwoSeqMissTen\% had never been returned. The last label alone is a poor guide, because a \lbl{changed} hop rewrites the stored quotation: it failed to signal \bTwoSeqMissLastTen\% of the changes since the original. In a Flask trace \cite{flaskchaincase}, the fuzzy tier moves a negative assertion's anchor onto a nearby positive one at hop~7, and later \lbl{content-match} labels certify only that the wrong code did not change again.

The identity rule closes this gap (Table~\ref{tab:identity}). With the default cascade it reduced the changes since review that the tenth label failed to signal from \wiChIOffMissLastTenK{} to \wiChIOffIdMissLastTenK{}, the number never returned at any hop, and the endpoints on wrong code under a final label requesting nothing from \wiChIOffSilentLastTenK{} to \wiChIOffIdSilentLastTenK{}. At the calibrated $\theta=\wpaLopoTheta$ the tenth label alone failed on \wiChICaliMissLastTenK{} changes, because the lower threshold accepts more fuzzy matches and each one rewrites the stored quotation; with the rule \wiChICaliIdMissLastTenK{} remained. For CodeMapper replayed hop by hop the rule cut wrong endpoints after ten commits from \wiChICmSeqWrongTen\% to \wiChICmSeqIdWrongTen\% and raised lost ones from \wiChICmSeqLostTen\% to \wiChICmSeqIdLostTen\%, so uncertain transitions became visible losses instead of silent drift; with IG+AGi no change since review went unreturned at any hop (\wiChICmSeqGiMissCumTenK{}, against \wiChICmSeqMissCumTenK{} with labels only). The floor of 0.5 was not tuned. Replaying every floor from 0.3 to 1.0 on the stored traces, unsignaled changes stayed at \wzDepMissRange{} for the deployed cascade and \wzGicMissRange{} for the calibrated one at every floor, because a request is computed against $q_0$ whether or not the anchor is kept; the floor only trades wrong endpoints for lost ones (at 1.0 the deployed cascade's \wzDepFFiveWrong{} wrong became \wzDepFTenWrong{} and its \wzDepFFiveLost{} lost \wzDepFTenLost). No floor separates the cases: on \lbl{changed} hops the similarity to $q_0$ had a median of \wzDepRatioTrueMed{} when the window was the true target and \wzDepRatioWrongMed{} when it was not. Recomputed on the same chains, CodeMapper returned different regions for identical inputs on \wiChIGateChains{} of 4,000 chains; all its rows in Table~\ref{tab:identity} come from one run.

\textbf{Supervisor interventions.}\label{sec:interventions} The replay assumes that nobody acts on a request. A simulated supervisor acting at commits 1, 2, 3 and 5, and only on a request, may correct a wrong or lost anchor to the reference location, accept a correctly placed \lbl{changed} anchor as reviewed, do whichever fits, or dismiss the issue (Table~\ref{tab:interventions}); changes are counted since the last review. With no action the simulator reproduced the stored cascade traces exactly and CodeMapper's on \wvGateCmAgree{} of \wvGateCmHops{} hops, because CodeMapper is not fully deterministic. Correction repairs placement: for the deployed cascade it raised correct endpoints by \wvDepDPOneCorrectAbs{} of \wvChains{} (95\% interval \wvDepDPOneCorrectAbsLo--\wvDepDPOneCorrectAbsHi) with \wvDepPOneAct{} actions, mostly by recovering lost anchors, but removed only \wvDepDPOneWrongAbs{} of \wvDepPZeroWrong{} wrong endpoints, because most of its wrong placements raise no request. Acceptance cannot repair a label: without the identity rule it lowered missed changes by exactly the changes it had reviewed (\wvDepDPTwoMissAbs{} for the cascade, \wvCmDPTwoMissAbs{} for CodeMapper+IG+AGi). With the rule the accepted code becomes the reviewed quotation, so acceptance removed \wvDepIdDPTwoReqAbs{} and \wvCmIdDPTwoReqAbs{} later requests on code already seen, with missed changes unchanged at \wvDepIdPTwoMiss{} and \wvCmIdPTwoMiss. Dismissal at the first request ended \wvDismissedMin--\wvDismissedMax{} chains per configuration, and in \wvDismissedChangedMin--\wvDismissedChangedMax{} of them the code changed afterwards; a dismissal hides such changes by design.

\subsection{What the Layer Costs}\label{sec:costs}
The layer converts silent failures into requests, and the price is paid on preserved code: over CodeMapper, false re-checks rose from \wiStuCmQFalse\% to \wiStuCmGiFalse\% on the student repositories and from \wiFrCmQFalse\% to \wiFrCmGiFalse\% on the stress seed. Whether that pays depends on the share $p$ of carried issues on changed code, which no study here measured. With $w$ the cost of a missed re-check in unnecessary confirmations, $\Delta_m$ the fall in the missed rate and $\Delta_f$ the rise in the false rate, the guarded configuration costs no more than the unguarded one when $p \ge \Delta_f/(\Delta_f + w\,\Delta_m)$. For the cascade this break-even share is \wiRealBreakEvenOne\% on real histories, \wiStuBreakEvenOne\% on the student repositories and \wiHumBreakEvenOne\% on the human-annotated regions at $w=1$, and \wiRealBreakEvenTen\%, \wiStuBreakEvenTen\% and \wiHumBreakEvenTen\% at $w=10$; for CodeMapper, whose labels alone miss little, it is \wiRealCmBreakEvenOne\%, \wiStuCmBreakEvenOne\% and \wiHumCmBreakEvenOne\% at $w=1$ and \wiRealCmBreakEvenTen\%, \wiStuCmBreakEvenTen\% and \wiHumCmBreakEvenTen\% at $w=10$. These points ignore silent misplacements, which favors the unguarded configuration. The only external figure, \wpeOutdatedPct\% of open-source review threads outdated by GitHub, counts changes anywhere in a diff hunk \cite{github2018outdating}, so it is only a rough ceiling. On the human-annotated regions the cascade's break-even at $w=1$ lies above even that ceiling.

\subsection{Choosing a Configuration on Unseen Projects}\label{sec:policy}
A similarity threshold tuned on some projects did not transfer to others (Section~\ref{sec:location}), so we asked the question at the level of whole deployment policies. Every recorded locator and layer was a candidate; the policy with the lowest cost $C=w(\text{missed}+\text{silent})+m\,\text{misdirected}+\text{lost}+\text{unnecessary}$ per carried target on eight real-history projects was evaluated on the ninth, and likewise over the student repositories (post hoc, tier E1). Selection beat the deployed cascade on all \wzRealThreeFolds{} held-out projects at every weight and on \wzStuThreeBeatDep{} of \wzStuThreeFolds{} student repositories at $w=m=3$, but not a strong fixed default: against CodeMapper+IG+AGi it won on \wzRealThreeBeatCm{} of \wzRealThreeFolds{} projects at $w=m=3$ and \wzRealTenBeatCm{} at $w=10$, and the folds chose \wzRealThreeDistinct{} and \wzRealTenDistinct{} different policies. A course gains most by replacing a weak locator and keeping the layer.

\textbf{Answer to RQ2.} Missed re-checks are rare on real histories and come from identifiable mechanisms: identical copies next to the original or added by the same revision, hidden indentation changes, and, on applied suggestions, edits around an unchanged quotation, which no quotation check can see. The quotation labels remove most of a locator's misses on natural code; the guards reduce what remains on every locator tested and turn most silent copy and indentation failures into requests, but they do not fix placement. Neither model's own judgment of change was a safe signal; a question about the concern predicted applied suggestions better, a proxy that does not establish usefulness to supervisors. Keeping the reviewed quotation reduced the changes since review that went unsignaled across ten commits, to none over CodeMapper with the guards, with or without supervisor action, within the limits of the replayed reference. All of this costs confirmations on preserved code, and whether it pays depends on a share of changed issues that a course would have to measure.
